Understanding the structure

What is a management services organization?

An MSO is a separate business that owns and operates the non-legal side of a law firm — billing, technology, marketing, procurement, HR, and administration. The law firm continues to exist as its own entity, owned by licensed attorneys, and the two are connected by a long-term services agreement. The structure has been common in medicine and dentistry for decades and has moved into legal services more recently.

Is Finch buying my law firm?

No. Your firm remains a separate, attorney-owned entity. What Finch acquires is the administrative and business infrastructure — the vendor contracts, the systems, the back office functions — along with the right to service your practice going forward. You are not selling your practice, your client list, or your license.

Does the firm keep its name?

Yes. Your name, your letterhead, your reputation in the community, and the way your firm presents itself to clients stay exactly as they are. Finch operates behind the scenes. We have no interest in consolidating firms under a single brand — the local identity you have built is one of the reasons your practice works.

Control and independence

Who decides which cases we take and how we handle them?

You do, entirely. Case selection, legal strategy, staffing decisions on individual matters, settlement posture, and every other exercise of professional judgment stay with the firm's attorneys. Finch does not practice law, does not employ attorneys to practice law, and has no voice in the substance of your work.

What changes for our attorneys and staff?

Attorneys stay with the firm. Administrative and support personnel typically transition to Finch, which usually means access to better benefits and a real HR function. Day to day, they keep doing the same work with the same people, in the same location. The main change most firms notice is that hiring, payroll, and benefits administration stop being your problem.

How are duration and exit provisions handled?

Every services agreement sets out defined terms for duration, performance expectations, and the mechanics available to either side. As a rule, we walk through those provisions line by line early in diligence — not because we expect to use them, but because our alignment is fundamental to the relationship.

We are building long-term, trust-based partnerships. We are straightforward about every aspect of how the MSO structure works: the economics, the governance, the obligations on both sides, and the exit terms. We do not consider an agreement ready to sign until every provision has been walked through with all parties.

Economics

How is the Practice Affiliation Fee determined?

It is a multiple of your firm's revenue, determined by firm size, profitability, practice mix, client concentration, and growth trajectory. We will provide an indicative range early in our conversations, and a concrete number following diligence.

Do all of the partners have to participate?

Yes. Finch becomes your firm's back office in full, which means the commitment runs both ways and across the entire ownership group. We are taking on the operational infrastructure of the whole practice, not a portion of it, and that only works when every partner is aligned behind the same arrangement.

Partners naturally arrive at the conversation from different places — one may be years from retirement while another is early in their career — and the structure accounts for those differences in how proceeds are allocated across the ownership group. What it does not accommodate is partial participation. Before anything moves forward, we want the entire partnership at the table and comfortable with the decision.

How will the Practice Affiliation Fee be taxed?

The Practice Affiliation Fee is structured to be taxed at long-term capital gains rates rather than as ordinary income. You are transferring the business assets and administrative infrastructure of the practice, not earning additional fee income, so the proceeds are treated as the sale of a capital asset. At current federal rates, that distinction is a meaningful difference in what you actually keep.

The precise structure depends on your firm's entity type, ownership makeup, and jurisdiction, and we walk through those mechanics in depth during our initial conversations. We work alongside your CPA throughout the process and encourage you to have your own tax advisor confirm the treatment for your specific circumstances.

Does Finch set or cap attorney compensation?

No. Attorney base compensation is agreed to before the start of each fiscal year through direct, frank conversation between the firm's partners and Finch leadership. It is not capped, and it is not set unilaterally.

Some MSOs position their upfront payment as a predictable advance against unpredictable future profit distributions. We do not structure it that way. Keeping incentives aligned between the firm's partners and Finch is our highest priority — we are working toward the same outcome you are: a larger, more efficient, more profitable practice. The upside belongs to the attorneys who generate it, and you hold more of it than we do.

Ethics and client matters

Does this comply with the rules of professional conduct?

The MSO structure is built specifically to respect the prohibitions on fee-sharing with non-lawyers, non-lawyer ownership of law firms, and interference with professional judgment. Because those rules vary by state, the analysis is jurisdiction-specific, and we structure each engagement with counsel experienced in this area. We encourage every firm we speak with to have its own ethics counsel review the arrangement independently.

Do we have to tell our clients?

Your engagement with clients does not change — same firm, same attorneys, same representation. Whether any disclosure is required depends on your jurisdiction's rules and the particulars of your arrangement. This is a question worth resolving with your ethics counsel before closing rather than after.

What happens to client files and confidential information?

Client files remain the property of the law firm and stay under attorney control. Where Finch personnel or systems touch client information incidentally — through billing or document management, for instance — access is governed by confidentiality obligations and access controls built to support your ethical duties, not to work around them.

Process and timing

How long does this take?

From an initial conversation to closing is typically 90 days. The sequence runs through an introductory call, an indication of terms, financial and operational diligence, and then definitive documentation. The pace depends mostly on how quickly your firm can provide diligence materials and how many decision-makers need to reach agreement.

What does Finch actually take over first?

Marketing and intake support come online early, because that is where firms feel the gap most acutely. Vendor consolidation and procurement savings follow. Deeper back office integration — billing systems, technology, reporting — happens over the first several months rather than all at once. Our sequencing revolves around a commitment to never disrupt the work or operations of your practice. We implement the most value-additive functions first, and save the longer-horizon infrastructure work for last.

What should we have ready before a first conversation?

Nothing. A first call is a conversation about your practice, your goals, and how our structure can best support you — it is not a diligence exercise. Items that are helpful include two or three years of rough financials, a sense of your revenue mix by practice area, and your firm's current back-office and administrative framework.
